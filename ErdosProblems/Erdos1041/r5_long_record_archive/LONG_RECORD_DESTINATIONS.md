## Failure map: what is insufficient and what would cross the boundary

**Sparse appearance without a controlled partial-sum polygon.** The exact
sextic leaves a prescribed root spoke. The Abel identity remains true; what
fails is automatic containment of its additional control points. A selected
convex certificate, or another path, is required.

**Low critical magnitude or a single separated value as an exhaustive
alternative.** The registered cubic falsifies the separation-two disjunction.
The new high-critical family additionally fails every radius-4/3 centre test
while admitting explicit short connections. The sufficient conditions are
valuable but do not exhaust root geometry.

**First-merge arity without later merger data.** The existing cubic reaches a
full-degree ancestor at the later working level. Track the actual component
and its intervening critical levels; arity alone does not supply a capacity
deficit.

**One-root membership as a perimeter bound.** The central component of
`z^8-(3/2)z` disproves the proposed gamma constant. The asymptotic family forces
any universal homogeneous perimeter constant of that form to be at least
`2*pi`; it does not prove `2*pi` is an upper bound.

**Angular L1 control without attachment.** For a binomial the usable angular
window may have measure `2*arcsin((epsilon/r)^n)`. A direction selected by
averaging need not be compatible with the desired root pair. Condition on
incidence or construct a competitor with a free attachment choice.

**A stronger marginal critical-value moment.** The new degree-24 witness and
high-critical asymptotics prevent increasing the degree-uniform numerator
above `4s`, even after excluding low mu and the specified separated regime.
A joint statement involving values, inverse sheets and metric cost is a
different possibility and is not refuted.

**Unqualified perturbation closeness.** Exponentially small coefficient
changes may change the closed-level root-pair distance by a fixed amount when
degree varies. The new exact limit is compatible with fixed-degree lower
semicontinuity. Include degree and a level/attachment scale, or use compactness
only in its valid fixed-degree direction.

This map is not a list of reasons to abandon the problem. It identifies
which hypotheses a successor theorem must actually strengthen.
